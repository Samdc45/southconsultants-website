Why a VOC, and why now

Under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, the business running the site must be able to show that each operator is competent on the machine and task they are doing — not just that they hold a licence or did a course years ago. Tier 1 clients and councils increasingly ask for that evidence at pre-qualification and audit.

A licence endorsement covers driving on the road. A VOC covers what actually happens on your job: trenching near services, working near power lines, lifting with an excavator, loading trucks, working on slopes and soft ground.

How it works

  • Plan — we agree the machines, tasks and site conditions to verify, and check prerequisites (licence, logbook hours or employer confirmation of experience).
  • Verify — on your site, on your machine: pre-start and routine checks, set-up and exclusion zones, operating to the standard your job needs, and shutdown. Oral questions cover services, power lines, trench safety, lifting and ground conditions.
  • Record — a signed VOC record per operator and machine, notes on any gaps, and a recommended re-verification date.
  • Close the gaps — where something needs work, we coach it on the spot or set up targeted training, so operators come out better, not just checked.

Frequently asked questions

Is a VOC the same as a WTR endorsement?

No. A W, T or R endorsement is an NZTA licence requirement for driving special-type vehicles on a public road. A VOC verifies that an operator is competent on a specific machine and task on your site — the evidence an employer needs under the Health and Safety at Work Act.

How long does it take?

Typically 45–90 minutes per operator per machine. A crew of four can usually be done in a half day without anyone leaving site.

What do we get at the end?

A completed, signed VOC record for each operator and machine, gap notes and a re-verification date — ready for your H&S file and client pre-qualification.

Is a VOC an NZQA qualification?

No. A VOC is workplace verification, not NZQA credits. If an operator wants a formal qualification, such as unit standard 17310 for excavators, we can point them to the right pathway.
